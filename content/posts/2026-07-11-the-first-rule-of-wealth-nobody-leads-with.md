title: The First Rule of Wealth Nobody Leads With
date: 2026-07-11

excerpt: Before allocation, before compounding, before any of it — the actual first move, and why it's boring on purpose.
---

Every wealth guide leads with the exciting part: index funds, compounding, asset allocation, the chart that goes up and to the right. Almost none of them lead with the part that actually determines whether you get to use any of it.

The first rule is this: know your number before you know your strategy. Not your net worth number — your *burn* number. What it actually costs you, per month, to live the life you already have. Most people can't answer this within twenty percent. That's not a knowledge problem, it's an avoidance problem, and it's the single biggest predictor of whether the rest of the advice ever gets applied.

Once you have that number, everything downstream gets simpler:

1. You know exactly how much of a bad month you can absorb before it becomes a bad year.
2. You know what "enough" would even look like, instead of chasing a moving target.
3. You stop optimizing a portfolio you haven't actually earned the right to worry about yet.

None of this is clever. It's a spreadsheet and an evening. That's exactly why it gets skipped — it doesn't feel like progress, it just is progress.
