---
title: On Writing in Public
date: 2026-03-15
excerpt: The first draft is never for anyone else. The second one might be.
---

Most advice about writing online skips the part that actually matters: the first draft is not for your reader. It's for you, working out what you think by watching yourself say it.

The mistake is publishing that draft. The second mistake is never writing it at all, because you were waiting to already know what you meant.

A method that works, roughly:

1. Write the bad version fast, with no audience in mind.
2. Leave it alone for a day.
3. Come back and cut everything that was you finding the idea, not stating it.

What's left is usually short. That's fine. Short and clear beats long and impressive almost every time, and it's much closer to the truth of what you actually think.

> Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication — but only after you've earned it by going through the complicated version first.

Nobody skips the complicated version. They just don't publish it.
