The Film That Recalibrated My Clock

There's a scene, about two-thirds through, where the main character does nothing for four full minutes. No dialogue, no score, just a person sitting with a decision they already know they've made. Most films would have cut it down to fifteen seconds. This one trusts you to sit in it.

That's the whole argument of the movie, really: most of what matters happens in the parts we're trained to skip.

I walked out and canceled two meetings I didn't need to be in.

Worth watching for:

  • The four-minute scene, obviously
  • A supporting performance that says more with posture than the leads say with lines
  • An ending that refuses to resolve the one thing you want resolved

Not worth watching if you need plot momentum every ten minutes. This isn't that kind of film, and it isn't trying to be.
