A Note on This Design

The layout on this site is deliberately asymmetrical. The headline doesn't sit in the center of the page — it's pushed to one side, anchored to a grid, with a single graphic mark placed against it like a counterweight. Nothing here is centered because centered is the default, and the default doesn't ask you to look twice.

This is an old idea. Swiss graphic designers in the mid-twentieth century built entire systems around it: a strict underlying grid, used not to constrain expression but to make asymmetry legible rather than arbitrary. Off-center, but not random — every position on this page answers to the same twelve-column structure.

The type is a neo-grotesque sans for anything structural — titles, navigation, dates — because that family of typeface (Helvetica and its relatives) was built to disappear and let the hierarchy do the talking. The body text you're reading now is a serif instead, chosen for long-form reading rather than signage. Two jobs, two typefaces.

And the color: one solid, saturated, slightly uncomfortable green. Not decoration — contrast. Stark black text on a field that vibrates a little at the edges of your vision. It's meant to be seen once and remembered, the way a good cover should be.

None of this is subtle. That's also the point.
